Public Citizen Classification

Understanding AQI Categories

The official National Air Quality Index (NAQI) classifies ambient air into 6 color-coded categories. Here is what every level means in simple, plain words and what actions you should take to protect your family.

AQI 0 - 50

Good

Minimal Impact

What it Means in Simple Words

Clean, pristine atmospheric air. The concentration of toxic particles is nearly zero and harmless.

Possible Health Impact

No health risk to any segment of society. Safe for newborns, pregnant mothers, seniors, and active athletes.

Recommended Citizen Action

  • Open windows freely for natural ventilation.
  • Great day for outdoor sports, morning jogging, and picnics.
  • No mask or air filtration required.
AQI 51 - 100

Satisfactory

Minor Discomfort

What it Means in Simple Words

Acceptable air quality. Meets most general safety criteria with faint background urban haze.

Possible Health Impact

Healthy adults notice nothing. A very small fraction of people with extreme asthma might feel mild throat dryness.

Recommended Citizen Action

  • Completely fine for normal outdoor activities.
  • Unusually sensitive asthmatics should observe mild cough.
  • Ventilate rooms during pleasant afternoon hours.
AQI 101 - 200

Moderate

Noticeable Discomfort

What it Means in Simple Words

Pollution is elevated. Noticeable urban smog or dust haze is visible against sunlight.

Possible Health Impact

Breathing discomfort to children, elderly seniors, and people with lungs, asthma, and heart conditions.

Recommended Citizen Action

  • Children & elderly reduce prolonged heavy exertion.
  • Asthma patients keep quick-relief inhalers nearby.
  • Close windows during morning and evening rush hours.
AQI 201 - 300

Poor

Unhealthy for Most

What it Means in Simple Words

Heavy air pollution. Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) hangs thick in the lower atmosphere.

Possible Health Impact

Causes breathing discomfort to most citizens on prolonged outdoor exposure. Frequent coughing and eye burning.

Recommended Citizen Action

  • Wear an N95 mask when stepping outdoors.
  • Cancel morning outdoor jogging and school sports.
  • Keep residential doors and windows closed.
AQI 301 - 400

Very Poor

Severe Health Hazard

What it Means in Simple Words

Hazardous chemical and particulate smog. Breathing outdoor air is equivalent to passive smoking multiple cigarettes.

Possible Health Impact

Significant respiratory illness on exposure. Severe distress for people with asthma, bronchitis, or heart disorders.

Recommended Citizen Action

  • Strictly avoid all outdoor physical exercise.
  • Children, pregnant women, and elderly must stay indoors.
  • Turn on HEPA air purifiers inside bedrooms.
AQI 401 - 500+

Severe

Public Health Emergency

What it Means in Simple Words

Emergency smog disaster. Visibly suffocating atmosphere with severe eye stinging and throat burning.

Possible Health Impact

Extremely dangerous. Directly affects healthy individuals with acute bronchitis, and causes severe cardiac strain in vulnerable patients.

Emergency Citizen Protocol

  • Health emergency! Complete outdoor curfew recommended.
  • Seal exterior window cracks with damp towels.
  • If stepping out is unavoidable, wear tightly sealed N95/N99.
