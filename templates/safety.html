Actionable Protection Protocols

Citizen Air Safety & Protection Guide

Tailored health guidelines for every member of your family. Learn how to minimize toxic particulate inhalation indoors and outdoors.

General Public

Everyday safety habits for commuters and working adults.

  • Avoid Rush-Hour Commutes: Traffic intersections contain 3x higher concentrations of ultrafine exhaust soot. Keep car windows rolled up.
  • Smart Home Ventilation: Open windows only between 12:00 PM and 4:00 PM when sunlight warms the ground and dilutes smog.
  • Hydration & Diet: Drink plenty of warm water, jaggery (gur), and vitamin-C rich citrus fruits to soothe inflamed throat airways.

Children & Parents

Children breathe 50% more air per kg of body weight than adults.

  • Suspend Morning Sports: Cancel outdoor school assemblies and physical training when AQI exceeds 200. Early mornings have toxic temperature inversions.
  • Fitted Pediatric Masks: Standard adult masks leave loose cheek gaps. Provide kids with certified pediatric N95 masks for bus transit.
  • Indoor Play Activities: Replace outdoor park play with indoor puzzles, reading, and gymnastics during high pollution alerts.

Elderly Seniors

PM2.5 particles enter the bloodstream, elevating heart attack risks.

  • No Early Morning Walks: Postpone daily walks until after 10:00 AM when the sun breaks cold ground-level smog layers.
  • Monitor Blood Pressure: Air pollution triggers vascular constriction. Check blood pressure regularly during winter smog spikes.
  • Medication Adherence: Keep prescribed cardiovascular and respiratory tablets stocked for at least 15 days.

Outdoor Workers & Athletes

Delivery riders, traffic police, construction staff, and runners.

  • Mandatory N95 with Exhalation Valve: Heavy exertion increases ventilation 5x. An exhalation valve reduces moisture buildup.
  • Shift Rotations: Employers must rotate staff between high-traffic roadside duty and shaded/indoor staging stations.
  • Frequent Eye Washing: Rinse eyes with clean sterile water after outdoor delivery shifts to remove corrosive acidic particulate dust.

Asthma & COPD Patients

Vulnerable individuals with chronic lung ailments or allergies.

  • Inhaler Readiness: Keep rapid-relief bronchodilator inhalers (e.g. Salbutamol) in your pocket whenever traveling.
  • Steam Inhalation: Inhale pure saline steam for 5 minutes before bedtime to clear sticky mucus and particulates from bronchioles.
  • Red Flag Symptoms: Immediate medical consultation if you experience persistent wheezing, chest tightness, or blue lips.

Housing Societies & Schools

Community-level mitigation and institutional precautions.

  • Zero Waste Burning: Strictly prohibit the incineration of dry leaves, plastics, or garbage in residential colonies.
  • Road Water Sprinkling: Spray water on unpaved internal society pathways to prevent fugitive dust resuspension.
  • Issue Emergency Notices: Use our Advisory Generator to broadcast printable society alerts.

Natural Plants & Indoor Air Purification

According to the NASA Clean Air Study, specific indoor houseplants naturally absorb toxic benzene, formaldehyde, and carbon monoxide from living spaces.

Snake Plant (Sansevieria)

Releases oxygen at night! Ideal for bedrooms to absorb toluene, xylene, and formaldehyde.

Spider Plant

Extremely resilient plant that actively captures carbon monoxide and household smoke fumes.

Areca Palm

Acts as a natural room humidifier while filtering out chemical solvents from paints and cleaning sprays.
